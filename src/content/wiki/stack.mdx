When you cast an Incantation, activate an ability, or a triggered keyword goes off, it goes on **the Stack**. Your opponent may respond. The Stack resolves **last in, first out**: the newest effect resolves first.

<StackDiagram />

If an effect's target is gone by the time it resolves, the effect **fizzles**.

## Spell speed

- **Speed 1**: Summons, Hex Spells, Items, Buildings, Terrains, Treasures. Only in your own Tactic Phase, with nothing else on the Stack.
- **Speed 2**: **Quick** Spells, and the activated abilities of Summons, Guardians, Items and Buildings. Any time you have priority, including in response to something.
- Two exceptions are always speed 1: **Movement Abilities** (Retreat, Switch and the like), and **Spells printed on a Summon** unless the Spell itself is Quick.

## Priority

Putting something on the Stack **hands priority to your opponent**. If an action doesn't use the Stack, you simply keep going. That's why ordinary play flows quickly.

**These never use the Stack:**

- playing a Worker or an Event
- setting a Trap
- generating Energy
- drawing from your Treasure Chest

**Triggered keywords use the Stack:** Manifest, Demise, Resonance, Assault, Onslaught, Ward, Dominate, Agility and Infiltrate. **Skills don't**: Retaliate, Goad, Swiftstrike, Swift, Shockwave and the like are simply part of the Summon.

## Casting a Spell, step by step

1. **Announce** it: choose its mode and the value of X.
2. **Choose targets.**
3. **Pay** its costs, including anything sacrificed as an additional cost.
4. It's on the Stack and your opponent may respond.

Nobody can respond in the middle of casting, and a cost already paid can't be undone. What you target can't also be what you sacrifice for it.

## Combat and the Stack

An attack is a sequence of steps, and players can respond in between: after the attack is declared, around the damage, around the counterattack, and at the end of combat.
